## Scope and notation

This is a derivation notebook about **conditioning, exponential tilting and generative policy extraction**. Its main paper is [Advantage Guidance v2, Sections 3–6 and Appendix A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19783v2#S3). The mathematical steps below are worked explanations; the finite discrete example and tests are notebook constructions, not benchmark results. It begins with one paper and can grow into a multi-paper concept note as further sources are actually read.

<div class="math-block" tabindex="0" role="group" aria-label="Equation; scroll horizontally if needed"><span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML" display="block"><semantics><mrow><mi>J</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>p</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>=</mo><mi>β</mi><mi>log</mi><mo>⁡</mo><mi>Z</mi><mo>−</mo><mi>β</mi><msub><mi>D</mi><mrow><mi mathvariant="normal">K</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">L</mi></mrow></msub><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>p</mi><mi mathvariant="normal">∥</mi><msup><mi>p</mi><mo>∗</mo></msup><mo stretchy="false">)</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">.</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">J(p)=\beta\log Z-\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|p^*).</annotation></semantics></math></span></div>

Nonnegativity of KL proves the optimum, rather than relying only on a stationary point. This requires <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>Z</mi><mo>&lt;</mo><mi mathvariant="normal">∞</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">Z&lt;\infty</annotation></semantics></math></span>; an unrestricted unbounded utility need not satisfy that condition. Where <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>q</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">q</annotation></semantics></math></span> is zero, this objective cannot create new support at finite KL. In practical neural policies, support may be formally broad while reliable offline-data coverage is narrow; those are not the same assurance.

At fixed state, adding a constant <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>c</mi><mo stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>s</mi><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">c(s)</annotation></semantics></math></span> to <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>A</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">A</annotation></semantics></math></span> changes <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><mi>Z</mi></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">Z</annotation></semantics></math></span> by the same multiplicative factor and leaves <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msup><mi>p</mi><mo>∗</mo></msup></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">p^*</annotation></semantics></math></span> unchanged. Multiplying advantage by a positive constant changes the effective temperature. These are useful invariance checks for both derivations and implementations.

The result is optimal for the **specified fixed-state surrogate objective**. It does not show that the critic is accurate, that the state visitation distribution is unchanged after deployment, or that the resulting policy globally optimizes an MDP.

## 4. Why the contrastive ratio cancels the quadratic term

For two labels, subtract their log likelihoods:

Suppose instead

Now the critical limitation: the Gaussian label model was specified given the **clean action**. Given an intermediate <span class="katex"><math xmlns="http://www.w3.org/1998/Math/MathML"><semantics><mrow><msub><mi>x</mi><mi>t</mi></msub></mrow><annotation encoding="application/x-tex">x_t</annotation></semantics></math></span>, the label likelihood averages over possible clean actions. The clean-action cancellation therefore does not automatically prove the same tilted marginal identity at every intermediate time.

Even a score assembled from an instantaneous product of marginals need not describe a consistent forward-noising path of the desired final density. The practical guided ODE must still be evaluated. In addition, learned velocity errors and Euler discretization introduce further error. These are separate from a sign mistake in the clean-density algebra.

The shared reference cancels inside the difference. Algorithm 1 integrates this velocity from Gaussian noise toward an action. ## 9. What is established, and what remains to test

There are four levels of evidence to keep separate:

1. **Variational identity:** the exponential tilt solves the stated fixed-state KL objective.
2. **Clean-score identity:** the contrastive ratio produces that score under the assumed likelihood model.
3. **Learned sampler:** neural approximation, intermediate-time guidance and numerical integration determine actual generated actions.
4. **Control performance:** the critic and environment determine whether those actions improve real return and satisfy constraints.

The first two do not certify the last two. A good next experiment holds a known utility fixed, varies integration steps and learned-field accuracy, and compares the generated distribution against an exactly normalized small reference problem. A second experiment perturbs the utility estimator while evaluating with the unperturbed utility. This separates sampler error from critic exploitation.
